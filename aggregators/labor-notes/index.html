Labor Notes

https://labornotes.org/

Movement documentsWorker powerLabor actionactive
The archive's reading. The archive reads Labor Notes as an organ independent of the union executives it criticizes, of the kind #1725 finds holding critique: a publication, a network and a conference through which a rank-and-file position keeps being stated and can be taken up later. In its own terms it exists to 'put the movement back in the labor movement' and promotes 'unions that are run by their members'.
Kinda media and organizing project: monthly magazine, website, books, conferences and workshops, and a network of rank-and-file members, local union leaders and labor activists
OperatorLabor Notes; the site footer names the Labor Education and Research Project as copyright holder
Unit of recordan article in the magazine or on the website; also a book or pamphlet, a conference, a Troublemakers School
What it recordsunion and workplace struggles from the side of union activists and rank-and-file members: organizing tactics, contract campaigns, strikes, fights over union democracy, and workers' struggles abroad, since 1979
Whose record it keepsthe voice of union activists and rank-and-file members, including their critique of union leaderships
Who holds itLabor Notes as an independent publication, supported by subscriptions, book sales and tax-deductible donations; reprints welcome with credit to author and Labor Notes
The losing sidekept as its sources show: an independent publication that states it is 'free to include the voices who say we could be doing better', whose about page criticizes union leaders ('doing a poor job of navigating us through the crisis'), and whose current stories report members contesting their own locals ('Members Alarmed by End-Run around Democracy at Buffalo Health Care Local'; 'UFCW Members Protect Local Election After Sham Merger Attempt'). Whether it keeps the record of positions after they lose is not shown in the saved pages
Methodreporting and writing by staff and contributing writers (writer guidelines, unread); books and pamphlets; biennial national conferences; Troublemakers Schools organized with local unions and community groups
Coveragegeography: United States mainly, with workers' struggles abroad; about a tenth of conference attendees from other countries; period: 1979 to the present
Countvalue: not stated as a count of articles or issues; it states tens of thousands of strategy manuals sold and hundreds of monthly donors; as_of: 2026-10-31
Holdingsthe monthly magazine Labor Notes (subscription $30 for 11 issues); articles online; books and pamphlets including A Troublemaker's Handbook 2, Democracy Is Power, Secrets of a Successful Organizer and How to Jump-Start Your Union; Robert M. Schwartz's Work Rights Press books; a national conference every two years; a podcast and the Troublemakers Blog; an Archives link in the footer (unread)
Stated limitsnone stated in the saved pages
Data accessarticles free online; magazine by subscription; books in the store; an archives section (unread)
Licencenot stated as a licence; the reprint policy asks credit to the author and Labor Notes and, online, a link to the original story
Lifecyclestate: active; detail: publishing since 1979; stories dated to 29 October 2026 on the home page; the about page still announces the June 12-14, 2026 Chicago conference as 'next', so that page is not current; the footer copyright reads 2005-2023
Founded1979

What its record can settle

  • how union activists and rank-and-file members describe a named campaign, strike or fight over union democracy
  • what tactics and strategies the rank-and-file current around Labor Notes recommends

What it cannot settle

  • counts of strikes, members or campaigns
  • the account given by the union leaderships it criticizes, except as its stories report them
  • what became of a dissenting position after it lost (not shown in the saved pages)

Kept apart

  • NOT a union: a media and organizing project and network
  • NOT a neutral record of labor: it states a position for member-run unions and against business-as-usual leadership
  • NOT a strike dataset
